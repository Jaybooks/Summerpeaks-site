Sales Tax Payable: Reconcile Before Filing

A sales tax return should tie back to documented transactions, not just the balance shown on the dashboard.

By Summer Peaks7 min readPublished October 26, 2026

A business can collect sales tax every day and still reach filing time with a liability balance that does not match the tax report. Refunds, exempt sales, marketplace transactions, timing differences and incorrectly mapped accounts can all create a gap. A monthly reconciliation makes that gap visible while the underlying records are still easy to find.

Sales tax rules vary by jurisdiction, product, service, customer, marketplace arrangement and filing period. This guide describes a bookkeeping control process, not a determination of where a particular business must register, collect or file.

Start with the right reports

Pull gross sales, taxable sales, non-taxable or exempt sales, sales tax charged, refunds and adjustments for the filing period. Separate channels such as point of sale, online store, invoices and marketplace sales. Export the sales tax liability account activity and beginning and ending balances from the general ledger. Keep the period and transaction cutoffs consistent across reports.

Reconcile activity before the liability balance

Compare the source system's tax collected with tax recorded in the books. Identify whether a processor settles sales net of fees or refunds; those cash settlement amounts are not necessarily the correct sales or tax base. Review credits issued after the original sale and determine whether they are reflected in the correct period under applicable rules.

Next, bridge the liability account: beginning payable plus tax collected and relevant adjustments, less tax remitted and valid reversals, equals ending payable. A return filed for one period and paid in another may leave a legitimate outstanding balance. Document it rather than forcing the account to zero.

Investigate common differences

  • Taxable versus exempt sales: Confirm classifications and retain applicable certificates or other support when required.
  • Refunds and credit memos: Check that the original tax component was reversed correctly and in the appropriate reporting period.
  • Marketplace sales: Identify sales for which a marketplace collects or remits tax, if applicable, and avoid duplicating liabilities.
  • Multiple locations: Confirm jurisdiction mapping, rate changes and destination rules using current official guidance.
  • Manual journal entries: Investigate entries posted directly to the tax liability account without a corresponding sales or payment record.
  • Use tax: Review purchases on which vendors did not charge tax separately from tax collected from customers.

The University of Washington's Procurement Services guidance distinguishes sales tax from use tax and emphasizes correcting tax treatment when a purchase is misclassified. Its examples concern Washington and university purchases; they should not be used as nationwide filing instructions.

Build a filing support package

Keep the final sales reports, liability rollforward, return workpapers, exemption documentation, adjustment approvals, filed return and payment confirmation together. Record who prepared the reconciliation, who reviewed it and when. Reconcile the payment back to the bank and general ledger after remittance. If a return is amended, preserve both versions and the reason for the change.

Keep tax decisions separate from bookkeeping entries

Accountants and business owners should verify current state and local requirements before choosing taxability, nexus, filing frequency or treatment of exemptions. Arizona, for example, uses a transaction privilege tax framework that should not be assumed identical to other states' sales taxes. Consult the relevant revenue agency or qualified sales tax specialist when the facts are uncertain.

A monthly review that scales

Use one checklist: confirm all selling channels, compare tax reports with ledger activity, explain differences, review outstanding liabilities, approve the return and retain proof of payment. Even a business with a small number of transactions benefits from a consistent trail. A clean reconciliation supports accurate reporting and a calmer filing process.
