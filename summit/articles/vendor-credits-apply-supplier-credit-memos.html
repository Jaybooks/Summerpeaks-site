Vendor Credits: Apply Supplier Credit Memos Without Losing Track

A supplier credit is valuable only when it is recorded, approved and actually applied or refunded.

By Summer Peaks7 min readPublished October 29, 2026

Supplier credits can quietly disappear between purchasing, accounts payable and the next check run. The controls below make credits traceable without erasing the original bill or paying a supplier twice.

Why vendor credits get lost

A supplier may issue a credit after a return, pricing correction, duplicate charge, service dispute, or agreed allowance. That credit is not necessarily cash in the bank. It may reduce a future invoice, offset an open bill, or be refunded separately. When the credit sits only in an email thread, the next payment run can overlook it and pay the full balance. A consistent accounts payable process makes the credit visible before money leaves the business.

Keep the supplier document and reason

Start with the vendor-issued credit memo or written confirmation. Record the vendor, credit number, issue date, original invoice or purchase order, amount, currency, reason, and any stated expiration or application terms. If the credit relates to returned goods, keep the return authorization and evidence that the supplier accepted the return. A verbal promise is a follow-up item, not a verified credit. Do not create a duplicate credit simply because the vendor sends a revised statement.

Confirm what the credit actually changes

Compare the credit with the original charge and the underlying purchase or service. Is the supplier correcting the price, reversing a returned item, refunding a deposit, or offering a goodwill allowance? The answer determines which bill and accounting period need review. Sales tax, inventory, expense classification, and other accounting treatment may differ by transaction and jurisdiction. Route uncertain items for accounting review instead of assuming every credit reduces current-month expense.

Enter the credit once and link it to the bill

Use the accounting system's vendor credit function when available, preserving the original invoice and the credit as separate records. Attach the supporting memo, use a clear reference number, and connect the credit to the intended vendor account. Before entry, search for the supplier credit number and amount to prevent duplicates. If the vendor has multiple entities or accounts, confirm which legal entity earned the credit. A credit issued to one company should not silently offset another company's payable.

Apply it before the next payment run

Review open vendor credits alongside the accounts payable aging and proposed payment list. Apply a valid credit to an eligible bill under the supplier's terms, and confirm that the remaining amount due agrees with the vendor statement. Do not simply reduce the check amount without updating the payable records; doing so can leave a misleading open balance. If the supplier promises a refund instead, track the expected refund separately and reconcile the actual bank receipt when it arrives.

Example: a credit on a future invoice

Suppose a supplier billed $900 for materials and later approved a $125 credit for an overcharge. If the original $900 bill remains unpaid and the credit is valid against it, the amount to settle is $775. If the original bill has already been paid and the supplier applies the credit to a later $600 invoice, the later payment would be $475. The correct workflow depends on the supplier's documentation and the accounting records; do not post both an offset and a refund for the same $125.

Reconcile open credits at month end

Build a short vendor-credit register showing opening unused balance, new credits, amounts applied, refunds received, and ending unused balance. Compare it with the accounts payable ledger and vendor statements. Investigate credits that are old, disputed, unmatched, issued to the wrong entity, or still expected as refunds. Assign an owner and next-action date. When a vendor statement does not show a credit already recorded internally, contact the supplier before releasing payment.

Keep approvals and audit evidence

The person approving a supplier dispute should not rely solely on an unverified email requesting changed bank instructions. Independently verify vendor payment-detail changes using an established contact channel. Retain the original bill, credit memo, supporting correspondence, approval, and evidence of application or refund. The IRS guidance on business records describes invoices and supporting documents as records that help substantiate business transactions. Retention periods and financial-reporting treatment depend on the circumstances.

A useful monthly checklist

Before closing accounts payable, confirm that each credit has a supporting supplier document, a unique reference, a correct vendor and entity, and an identified application or refund plan. Compare outstanding credits with the proposed payment run and the latest supplier statements. Review old balances, duplicate entries, disputed amounts, and expected refunds. Record who completed the review and which exceptions remain open.
