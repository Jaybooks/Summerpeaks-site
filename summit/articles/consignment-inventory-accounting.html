Consignment Inventory: Track Stock Held by Someone Else

Track consigned units by location, reconcile sales and payouts, and keep unsold goods visible in your inventory records.

By Summer Peaks5 min readPublished November 14, 2026

A retailer can hold your merchandise for weeks and owe you nothing for the unsold units. If the arrangement is a true consignment, shipping the goods to that retailer changes their location in your inventory records. It does not by itself create a sale.

Check the agreement before posting

Identify who controls the goods before a sale, who can require their return or transfer, and when the retailer becomes obligated to pay. Under ASC 606, indicators of consignment include continued control by the supplier until a specified event, the supplier’s ability to require return or transfer, and the dealer having no unconditional obligation to pay. Review the actual terms before applying this treatment.

Track the stock by location

Keep a schedule by partner and SKU. Start with opening units, add shipments and subtract units sold, returned to you, or removed through approved adjustments. Match the calculated ending quantity to the partner’s report or physical count. Use shipment and return records to explain differences before posting inventory adjustments.

Reconcile the sales report and payout separately

Use the partner’s sales report to identify units sold and the sale dates. Then compare the amount due under the agreement with the payout. List commissions, returns, and other deductions separately and challenge unexplained amounts. A payout may cover sales from several periods, so track the outstanding settlement separately from the inventory balance.

Work through the numbers

Suppose you ship 30 units costing $20 each. The retailer sells eight and returns two to you. It still holds 20 units worth $400 at your cost. The two returned units cost $40 and go back into your own stock, assuming they are saleable. The eight sold units account for $160 of cost of goods sold. The original $600 of stock is accounted for by $400 off-site, $40 returned and $160 sold.

If those eight units sold for $50 each and the agreement calls for a 25% commission on those sales, the expected payout is $300 before any other agreed deductions. Compare that amount with the remittance report and track any difference. Review the agreement and applicable revenue guidance when deciding how to present revenue and fees.

Close the month with evidence

Keep the signed agreement, shipment records, partner sales and inventory reports, and payout detail together. Follow up on stock with no recent count, sales missing from a settlement, and inventory reported as damaged or lost. Assign each exception an owner and a follow-up date.

Sources

The University of Texas at El Paso’s Inventory Services guidance illustrates keeping inventory responsibility with the lending department when it transfers physical custody. Its procedure concerns university equipment. FASB’s revenue recognition guidance, ASC 606-10-55-79 through 55-80, describes the control-based analysis and indicators of consignment. The numerical example is illustrative.
