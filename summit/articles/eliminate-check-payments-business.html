Accounts Payable & Payment Controls: Move Beyond Paper Checks Without Losing Control

A safer, faster payment workflow for AP and customer receipts using electronic payments, clear approvals, vendor verification, documentation and reconciliation.

Replacing paper checks can remove printing, mailing, depositing and tracking work, but speed is not the only objective. A modern payment process should also preserve authorization, documentation and fraud controls.

Start with the two payment flows

Outgoing AP: invoice received → documentation → approval → payment scheduled → payment released → bank reconciliation. Incoming AR: invoice sent → payment method offered → payment received → customer identified → payment applied → deposit reconciled.

Use electronic methods where they fit

ACH and other electronic payment methods can reduce paper handling for routine transactions. The right method depends on the business, customer/vendor preferences, cost and control requirements.

Centralize invoice intake

Vendor bills should enter through a predictable channel so the business can see what is owed before payment. Scattered inboxes create missed due dates, duplicate submissions and poor cash visibility.

Define approval before payment

Approval should answer: is the charge legitimate, is the amount correct, is supporting documentation present and is the payment timing authorized? The person releasing money should have enough evidence to understand what they are paying.

Treat bank-detail changes as high risk

Email compromise scams frequently attempt to redirect legitimate payments. A change in payment instructions should be independently verified using a known contact method rather than relying only on the message requesting the change.

Make customer payments easy to identify

Electronic receipts create cleanup when the payer or invoice is unclear. Ask customers to include the invoice number or another useful identifier and reconcile processor activity to gross customer transactions, fees, refunds and net deposits.

Schedule rather than react

A regular payment run can reduce emergency payments and help management compare bills due with cash available. Exceptions can still be handled, but the default process should be predictable.

Keep the bank reconciliation as the final control

Even a well-designed payment workflow can produce timing differences, duplicate entries or failed transactions. Reconciliation confirms that recorded payments and receipts agree with external bank activity.

Faster payments are useful. Controlled payments are better.

The best workflow removes paper and repetitive handling without removing the review that protects the business.
