Tax Extensions: When to File, Pay, and Finish the Return

A federal extension can buy more time to finish a return. In most cases, it does not buy more time to pay the tax.

Tax extensions are frequently misunderstood because two different deadlines are involved: the deadline to file a return and the deadline to pay tax. For most federal income-tax extensions, the IRS gives additional time to file the completed return, but the original payment deadline still applies.

This guide focuses on common federal filing rules for U.S. individuals and businesses. Exact dates can change because of weekends, legal holidays, fiscal tax years, special filing situations, and IRS disaster relief. State extension and payment rules are separate and should be checked with the applicable state tax agency.

The rule to remember: an extension to file is usually not an extension to pay

The IRS states this directly for both individual extensions and Form 7004 business extensions. If tax is expected to be due, estimate the liability using the best information available and pay by the original payment deadline. Filing the extension does not move that payment date merely because the completed return will be filed later.

Extension decision

Request an extension when the return cannot be completed accurately by its original filing deadline. Do not use the extension as a reason to postpone estimating or paying tax that is already due.

Individuals and sole proprietors

Most individual taxpayers use a calendar year. For 2025 federal individual income-tax returns, the IRS set the regular filing deadline at April 15, 2026. A taxpayer who needed more time generally had to request the automatic extension by that original deadline. A timely extension gives a calendar-year individual until October 15, 2026 to file the completed return.

A sole proprietor normally reports business activity on the owner's individual return, commonly using Schedule C with Form 1040. That means the individual Form 1040 extension framework generally controls the income-tax return deadline. The extension does not postpone the April 15, 2026 payment deadline for tax shown to be due for a calendar-year individual.

Fiscal-year individual filers do not simply use the April and October calendar-year dates. The IRS states that an individual return is generally due on the 15th day of the fourth month after the fiscal year ends, and Form 4868 is used to request an automatic six-month filing extension.

Partnerships

A domestic partnership generally files Form 1065. The IRS 2026 tax calendar states that Form 1065 is due on the 15th day of the third month after the partnership's tax year ends. A calendar-year partnership's 2025 return therefore fell in March 2026; because March 15, 2026 was a Sunday, the regular deadline moved to the next business day, March 16.

Form 7004 is generally used to request an automatic filing extension and must normally be filed on or before the return's due date. The general Form 7004 extension period is six months, making September 15, 2026 the extended filing deadline for a calendar-year partnership that timely extended its 2025 Form 1065.

Partnerships are generally pass-through entities for federal income tax, so partners commonly pay tax on their shares through their own returns rather than the partnership paying federal income tax on the passed-through profit. That does not mean a partnership can ignore every possible entity-level tax or payment obligation; special taxes and circumstances can apply.

S corporations

An S corporation generally files Form 1120-S by the 15th day of the third month after its tax year ends. For a calendar-year S corporation filing its 2025 return, the weekend rule moved the regular 2026 deadline to March 16, 2026. A timely Form 7004 generally provides a six-month filing extension, so the extended deadline was September 15, 2026.

S corporations generally pass income, losses, deductions, and credits through to shareholders, but an S corporation can owe certain entity-level federal taxes in particular circumstances. Form 7004 does not extend the time to pay tax that the corporation itself owes.

C corporations

Most C corporations file Form 1120. The IRS states that the return of most C corporations is due on the 15th day of the fourth month after the tax year closes. A calendar-year C corporation's 2025 return was therefore generally due April 15, 2026. A timely Form 7004 generally gives a six-month filing extension, which makes October 15, 2026 the extended filing deadline for a calendar-year corporation in this common situation.

Fiscal-year corporations require more care. The due date is tied to the corporation's year-end, and special rules historically applied to C corporations with June 30 year-ends. Current Form 7004 instructions say that for tax years beginning in 2026, the automatic extension period for June 30 year-end C corporations is six months; IRS guidance also notes the transition in the underlying due-date rule. Use the instructions for the specific return and tax year instead of assuming a calendar-year deadline.

Quick reference for common calendar-year 2025 returns filed in 2026

  • Individual / sole proprietor: regular Form 1040 deadline April 15, 2026; timely extension generally moves filing to October 15, 2026, but not the original tax-payment deadline.
  • Partnership: 2025 Form 1065 deadline March 16, 2026 because March 15 fell on Sunday; timely extension generally moved filing to September 15, 2026.
  • S corporation: 2025 Form 1120-S deadline March 16, 2026 because March 15 fell on Sunday; timely extension generally moved filing to September 15, 2026.
  • Calendar-year C corporation: 2025 Form 1120 generally due April 15, 2026; timely extension generally moves filing to October 15, 2026.

These dates are a reference for the common calendar-year situations described above, not a universal tax calendar. Different tax years and special filing situations produce different dates.

What if the extension deadline has already passed?

File the completed return as soon as possible rather than waiting for another convenient date. An automatic extension generally must be requested by the applicable return's due date. If that opportunity was missed—or a valid extension has now expired—filing sooner can limit additional failure-to-file consequences where they apply.

As of September 16, 2026, the common September 15 extended deadline for calendar-year 2025 partnership and S corporation returns has passed. A business in that situation should determine its actual filing status and any special relief that applies, then complete the overdue filing promptly.

What if the return can be filed but the tax cannot be paid in full?

Do not hold an otherwise ready return merely because the full balance cannot be paid. The IRS advises taxpayers to file on time even when they cannot pay in full, pay as much as they can, and consider available payment options. Interest and applicable late-payment penalties can continue on unpaid balances.

The IRS offers online payment-plan options for eligible taxpayers. Other collection alternatives can exist depending on the taxpayer's circumstances. The important distinction is that a payment arrangement addresses the unpaid balance; it is not a substitute for filing the required return.

Disaster relief can change federal dates

The IRS can postpone filing and payment deadlines for taxpayers affected by qualifying federally declared disasters. Relief varies by disaster, location, tax action, and relief notice. Do not assume that a widely reported extension applies to a particular taxpayer. Check the IRS disaster-relief page and the notice covering the taxpayer's location and filing obligation.

State extensions are separate

A federal extension does not establish every state filing or payment deadline. States have their own rules about automatic extensions, forms, payments, and conformity with federal disaster relief. Confirm the applicable state rules directly rather than carrying a federal date into a state return.

Build the extension estimate from dependable books

An extension still requires financial information. Before estimating tax or sending tax-preparation data, reconcile bank and credit-card accounts, confirm income completeness, review major expenses and owner activity, tie payroll to payroll reports, and identify financing and fixed-asset activity.
