Outstanding Checks: What to Do When a Payment Never Clears

An old check is not extra cash. Keep the payment obligation visible until it is properly resolved.

By Summer Peaks6 min readPublished October 24, 2026

Your bank reconciliation shows a check that was issued months ago but has never cleared. The bank balance still includes the money, but the books already reflect the payment. It is easy to treat that difference as harmless, especially if the vendor has stopped asking about it. In reality, an aging outstanding check needs an owner and a documented resolution.

Understand what is outstanding

An outstanding check is a payment the business issued and recorded but the bank has not yet paid. This is usually a timing difference when the check is recent. A check that stays outstanding over multiple reconciliations becomes an exception requiring investigation. A bank reconciliation should retain the item until its status is explained, rather than simply deleting it to make the balance agree.

Build a short aging schedule

For each uncleared payment, capture the check number, issue date, payee, amount, bank account, original invoice or payroll reference, and last follow-up. Sort the list by age. A business can set internal review thresholds, such as investigating items after 30 or 60 days, without assuming those thresholds determine legal expiration. Review the schedule alongside the monthly bank reconciliation and compare it with the prior month's outstanding items.

Verify before reissuing

First confirm that the check has not cleared, including activity after the statement date. Contact the payee through a verified channel to ask whether it was received, lost or deposited. If replacement is appropriate, follow the bank's stop-payment process and confirm the original payment cannot be presented before issuing a replacement. Link the new payment to the original record so the business does not pay the same obligation twice.

Keep an approval trail showing who authorized a stop payment, void or reissue. A canceled or voided payment in accounting software does not, by itself, guarantee that a bank will reject the physical check.

Do not write off obligations casually

Old uncashed checks may fall under state unclaimed-property or escheatment rules, which vary by jurisdiction and payment type. Some payments require additional notice, reporting or remittance to a state. A check becoming stale under a bank's policy does not automatically extinguish the amount owed to the payee. Consult applicable state guidance or a qualified adviser before clearing old liabilities or treating the amount as income.

Make the monthly review repeatable

  • Reconcile every bank account and retain a dated list of uncleared checks.
  • Identify which checks are new, aging or already under investigation.
  • Confirm current bank status before contacting payees or authorizing replacements.
  • Document stop payments, voids, reissues and any unclaimed-property review.
  • Carry unresolved obligations forward with a named owner and next review date.

The University of Arkansas–Pulaski Technical College's bank reconciliation and outstanding-check policy illustrates the importance of monitoring reconciling items and retaining support. Florida A&M University's bank reconciliation procedures also show how older checks can move into payee follow-up and replacement review. These are examples of control practices, not universal legal deadlines.
