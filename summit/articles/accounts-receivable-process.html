Accounts Receivable: A Practical System for Getting Paid Faster

A practical AR system for faster billing, lower payment friction, weekly follow-up, cash application, escalation and better visibility into what customers owe.

Accounts receivable is not simply a list of unpaid invoices. It is a process that starts before the invoice is created and ends only when the payment is correctly applied. Businesses often focus on collections while the real delay is earlier: missing billing information, slow invoicing, unclear terms or an approval problem at the customer.

Step 1: Make the invoice easy to approve

Before the work begins, collect the legal customer name, billing contact, email or portal, purchase-order requirements and payment terms. An invoice that is technically correct but cannot enter the customer's approval workflow is already late.

Step 2: Tie billing to an operational trigger

Define exactly what causes an invoice to be created: completed service, shipment, milestone, recurring date or approved change order. The trigger should produce the invoice promptly without depending on someone remembering days later.

Step 3: Reduce payment friction

Provide clear remittance instructions and electronic payment options that fit the business. Make the invoice number and customer identity easy to include with payment so cash can be applied without research.

Step 4: Review AR every week

A useful weekly review separates balances into four buckets: not yet due, due soon, overdue with a clear next action, and disputed/blocked. Every material overdue balance should have an owner and a next date.

Step 5: Diagnose before escalating

Ask why the invoice is unpaid. Common causes include missing PO numbers, the wrong contact, a customer portal requirement, a dispute, unapplied cash or genuine credit risk. The response should match the cause.

Step 6: Create a consistent escalation ladder

Define what happens at 1, 7, 15, 30 and later days past due based on your customer relationships and credit policy. Consistency is more important than aggression. The process should also define when new work requires prepayment or management approval.

Step 7: Reconcile the cash application

Receiving money is not the end of AR if the payment is sitting unapplied or attached to the wrong invoice. Reconcile customer payments, merchant deposits and the AR subledger so aging reports remain meaningful.

The metrics worth watching

  • Total AR and percent current.
  • Balances over 30, 60 and 90 days.
  • Average time from work completion to invoice.
  • Days sales outstanding when the metric is meaningful for the business.
  • Disputed balances and the reason for dispute.
  • Unapplied cash.
  • Largest customer concentrations.

A 20-minute weekly AR meeting

  1. Review the ten largest overdue balances.
  2. Confirm the reason each is unpaid.
  3. Assign the next action and date.
  4. Review invoices that should have been sent but were not.
  5. Review unapplied payments and disputes.
  6. Update the short-term cash forecast using realistic collection dates.
The objective is not “collect harder.”

The objective is to remove avoidable delay from the entire path between completed work and usable cash.
