Build an Accounts Receivable Process That Gets You Paid Faster

A practical AR playbook for invoicing, payment terms, follow-up, visibility, and escalation.

Accounts receivable is not a monthly report. It is a operating process that starts before the invoice is created and ends only when the cash is correctly applied. A strong AR system reduces billing errors, makes collection work predictable, and gives management a more realistic view of near-term cash.

The fastest collection process starts before an invoice becomes overdue.

Correct billing contacts, purchase-order requirements, clear terms, accurate line items and easy payment options prevent avoidable delays.

1. Make the invoice easy to approve

Confirm the legal customer name, billing contact, purchase order, project reference, payment terms and required portal before the work is complete. Many “collection problems” are actually invoice-delivery or documentation problems.

2. Invoice as soon as the billing event occurs

For completed work, unnecessary billing delay extends the amount of time your business finances the customer. Build invoice creation into the job-close or service-completion workflow instead of treating it as a separate administrative task.

3. Make payment friction low

Electronic payment options can shorten the distance between approval and cash. The best choice depends on economics and customer preference, but the customer should not need to call you to learn how to pay.

4. Review AR every week

Use aging as an action list. Separate current balances, overdue invoices, disputes, missing documentation, promised payments and unapplied cash. Assign a next action and owner to material items.

5. Escalate consistently

Define what happens at due date, 7 days late, 15 days late, 30 days late and at any credit-hold or prepayment threshold. Consistency prevents the awkwardness of inventing a collection policy customer by customer.

6. Reconcile cash application

A customer can pay and still appear overdue if the payment is unapplied or attached to the wrong invoice. The AR subledger, merchant or bank deposit, and general ledger should tell the same story.

Metrics worth tracking

  • Total AR and past-due AR.
  • Percent current, over 30, over 60 and over 90 days.
  • Days sales outstanding, interpreted in the context of your terms and billing model.
  • Average time from completed work to invoice.
  • Dispute volume and common root causes.
  • Unapplied cash.

A simple weekly AR meeting

Spend 20 minutes on the ten balances most likely to affect cash. Ask: Is the invoice correct? Did the right person receive it? Is documentation missing? Is there a dispute? Has the customer promised a date? What happens next? The point is not a longer report. It is fewer balances with no owner and no next action.
