Collection problems often begin before an invoice becomes overdue. Clear terms, accurate billing, easy payment instructions, and defined ownership reduce avoidable friction.

Make the invoice easy to understand

Send invoices promptly with the correct customer name, purchase-order or reference information when required, service dates, amounts, due date, and payment instructions. Errors give customers a legitimate reason to delay processing.

Define the follow-up timeline

Decide what happens before the due date, on the due date, and at specific aging points afterward. Routine reminders can be standardized; material accounts and broken promises should receive deliberate human follow-up.

Separate disputes from nonpayment

A disputed invoice needs an owner and a resolution path. Track the reason, required correction, responsible person, and expected resolution date instead of letting the balance sit in ordinary aging.

Make ownership obvious

Define who creates invoices, who reviews aging, who contacts customers, who can approve credits, and when sales or management becomes involved. Ambiguous ownership is one of the easiest ways for old balances to be ignored.

Review AR as a management metric

Look beyond total receivables. Review aging, large concentrations, credits, unapplied cash, recurring disputes, and whether collections are keeping pace with sales. Rising AR can create cash pressure even while reported revenue grows.

AR is both accounting and operations.

The ledger records what is owed; the process determines whether the business collects it efficiently.
