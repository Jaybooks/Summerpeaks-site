More reports do not automatically create more insight. A useful package combines a few dependable financial views with comparisons and follow-up on material exceptions.

Profit & Loss

The income statement shows revenue and expenses over a period. Review changes in sales, gross margin when relevant, payroll, major operating costs, and unusual items—not just the bottom-line profit.

Balance Sheet

The balance sheet shows assets, liabilities, and equity at a point in time. It helps reveal cash, debt, receivables, payables, credit-card balances, and other obligations that the income statement cannot explain by itself.

Accounts Receivable Aging

For businesses that invoice customers, aging shows who owes money and how long balances have been outstanding. Review large concentrations, old balances, credits, unapplied payments, and disputes that need action.

Accounts Payable Aging

Payables visibility helps management understand upcoming vendor obligations, overdue bills, credits, and near-term cash needs. Old balances should be investigated rather than carried forward indefinitely.

Cash deserves its own view

Current cash should be considered with expected collections and upcoming obligations. A rolling short-term forecast can reveal timing pressure that neither profit nor a static balance sheet shows clearly.

Compare against something

Reports become more useful when compared with prior months, prior year, budget, forecast, or another relevant benchmark. The comparison directs attention to what changed and where management should ask why.

A report package should end with action.

Highlight the few changes, balances, or exceptions that require an owner decision rather than burying them inside a large packet.
