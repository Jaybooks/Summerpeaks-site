Business Process Automation: What to Automate First

Choose automation targets based on repetition, structure, error risk, handoffs, and measurable value.

The best first automation is usually not the flashiest process. It is a stable, repetitive workflow with clear inputs, clear ownership, and enough volume that removing manual handling creates measurable value.

Start with high-frequency work

A task performed hundreds of times a month can justify improvement even when each instance takes only a few minutes. Document volume and handling time before choosing the solution.

Look for structured decisions

Rules-based routing, reminders, document collection, status updates, and standardized data movement are stronger candidates than decisions that depend on context, negotiation, or professional judgment.

Avoid automating unclear ownership

If nobody can explain who owns the process, what counts as complete, or how exceptions are resolved, automation will preserve the confusion. Fix the operating model first.

Connect information instead of creating another silo

A new tool should reduce duplicate entry and improve visibility. If employees must copy the same information into another disconnected system, the automation may simply move the work.

Choose a measurable first win

Track a before-and-after measure such as cycle time, manual touches, error rate, overdue items, or hours spent. Use the result to decide whether the next workflow deserves investment.

Standardize, simplify, then automate.

Removing an unnecessary step is usually better than automating it.
