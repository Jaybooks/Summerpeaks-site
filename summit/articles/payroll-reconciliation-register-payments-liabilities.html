Payroll Reconciliation: Match the Register, Payments and Liabilities

A completed pay run and a cleared bank withdrawal answer different questions. Reconciliation connects both to the accounting records.

By Summer Peaks6 min readPublished October 18, 2026

Payroll can be processed correctly while the accounting records still need attention. A missing import, duplicate bank categorization or unmatched deduction payment can affect the books without changing what an employee received. A payroll reconciliation makes those differences visible rather than assuming a successful pay run proves every related account is correct.

1. Agree on which payroll you are reviewing

Identify the company, pay-run identifier, pay date and reporting period. Gather the final payroll register, payroll journal or accounting export, funding detail, relevant bank activity and remittance records. Include off-cycle runs, voids and corrections. Keep preliminary reports separate from final results.

Pay dates and the periods employees worked may differ. Use consistent date filters for the comparison, and document any period-end accrual separately. Otherwise, a timing difference may look like a missing transaction. Reconcile each legal entity independently before reviewing consolidated totals.

2. Match the register to the ledger

Compare the final payroll totals with the posted accounting entry. Review gross earnings, employee deductions and withholdings, employer payroll costs and net pay as distinct components. Reimbursements and provider fees may need separate accounting depending on their nature; they should not disappear into a single wages category merely because the provider collected them together.

Confirm that the entry posted once, to the correct company, accounts and period. If payroll is imported automatically, inspect the mapping rather than assuming the integration classified every item as intended. A change in a benefit or deduction code is a reason to revisit that mapping.

3. Explain the cash movements

A provider may collect net pay, taxes, benefits or fees through different withdrawals and on different dates. Use its funding report to connect those amounts to bank activity. Do not force a single bank withdrawal to equal gross wages when the underlying components differ.

Check whether imported payroll entries already account for the cash movement. Categorizing the same bank-feed withdrawal as a new expense can duplicate what was recorded through the payroll journal. Depending on the system's design, the appropriate action may be matching the existing entry or clearing a payroll funding account. Confirm the actual workflow before posting.

4. Prove what remains payable

Review each material payroll liability with its own supporting detail. Start with the opening balance, add obligations recorded during the period, subtract documented settlements and include supported corrections. The result should explain the closing ledger balance.

A remaining balance is not automatically an error. It may represent an amount not yet remitted. Conversely, a zero balance does not prove a payment reached the intended recipient. Trace settlements to the appropriate provider, agency or benefit administrator records, and investigate old balances or unexpected debit balances.

5. Keep an exception log and a review trail

For each difference, record the affected run or account, amount, explanation, evidence, person responsible and next action. Separate known timing items from unresolved errors. Do not post an unsupported adjustment simply to make the reconciliation agree.

Retain the completed review and evidence of approval. Where staffing permits, have someone other than the preparer review the reconciliation. Restrict access to employee-level reports and use a secure document channel rather than sending payroll files through a general website form.

Define the accounting scope clearly

Payroll processing, payroll bookkeeping, remittance verification and tax-return preparation are related but different responsibilities. Confirm which party performs each task and how corrections are escalated. Filing rules, deposit deadlines and treatment of particular benefits require current, situation-specific guidance; this article does not prescribe those requirements.

Connect this review with your bank reconciliation and month-end close, but keep the payroll supporting schedule available on its own. An owner should be able to see which runs were reviewed and what remains unresolved without reading every employee record.

Sources and limits

The University of Washington's general ledger reconciliation policy supports comparing ledger balances with supporting schedules and resolving differences. Its institutional timetable is not a deadline for private businesses. The North Carolina Office of the State Controller's payroll reconciliation policy illustrates review of payroll results and ledger postings within its state systems. These sources support the control principles, not a claim that their specific policies govern your business. The workflow above is general educational guidance, not tax or legal advice.
