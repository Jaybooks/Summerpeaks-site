How Much Does Outsourced Bookkeeping Really Cost?

What drives outsourced bookkeeping pricing, how to compare providers, and how to think about cost versus an internal hire.

The useful question is not “What does outsourced bookkeeping cost?” in isolation. It is “What does the business need done, how much cleanup or complexity exists, and what financial outcome should the work produce?” Two companies with the same revenue can require very different accounting effort.

What usually drives the scope

  • Transaction volume and number of accounts.
  • How current and reconciled the books are.
  • Number of entities, locations or revenue streams.
  • Payroll, inventory, job costing or marketplace complexity.
  • Accounts receivable and payable involvement.
  • Reporting and cash-forecast requirements.
  • Amount of historical cleanup.
  • Frequency and complexity of owner questions.

Compare cost to the alternative

A full-time employee includes wages plus employer taxes, benefits, recruiting, management time, software and the risk of having either too much or too little capacity. Outsourcing can be attractive when the business needs reliable accounting capability but not a full internal seat.

Beware of the cheapest quote

A low monthly price can be expensive if reconciliations are incomplete, reports arrive late, balance-sheet accounts are ignored, or the owner spends hours filling gaps. Define deliverables and timing before comparing price.

A better pricing conversation

Ask what is included in the monthly close, which accounts are reconciled, what happens to open questions, how AR/AP is handled, what reports are delivered, how cleanup is priced, and what triggers a scope change.

Summer Peaks approach

The scope should fit the work. A business that needs monthly bookkeeping should not pay for management reporting it will not use. A company that needs cash forecasting and AR control should not be forced into a bookkeeping-only package. Start with the constraint and price the useful scope.
