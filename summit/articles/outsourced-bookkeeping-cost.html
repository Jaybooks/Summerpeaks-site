What Outsourced Bookkeeping Costs—and What You Should Compare Before Hiring

How to compare outsourced bookkeeping proposals by scope, complexity, close quality, reporting, cleanup needs and the true cost of doing the work internally.

There is no honest universal price for outsourced bookkeeping because the workload can vary dramatically between two businesses with the same revenue. The useful question is not “what does bookkeeping cost?” It is “what work is included, what condition are the books in, and what result will I receive each month?”

What usually drives scope

  • Number of bank and credit-card accounts.
  • Transaction volume and complexity.
  • Whether accounts receivable or payable support is included.
  • Payroll and benefit accounting complexity.
  • Inventory, job costing, multiple entities or locations.
  • How far behind or inaccurate the current books are.
  • Reporting and management-analysis needs.
  • How organized source documents and workflows are.

Monthly bookkeeping and cleanup are different projects

Current monthly work is easier to scope when the opening balances are dependable. Catch-up or cleanup may require reconstructing reconciliations, correcting account structure, resolving old receivables/payables or tracing payroll and loan balances. Ask whether cleanup is separately defined so the ongoing price is not hiding a one-time problem.

Compare deliverables, not just hours

One provider may offer transaction coding only. Another may include reconciliations, month-end review, balance-sheet cleanup, financial statements and a management call. A lower price is not lower cost if the owner still has to finish the accounting.

Compare against the real internal alternative

An employee costs more than wages alone and also requires recruiting, payroll taxes, benefits where applicable, supervision, software and enough consistent work to justify the seat. Outsourcing can be attractive when the business needs real accounting support but not a full internal workload.

Ask these questions before accepting a proposal

  1. What exactly is included every month?
  2. Which accounts are reconciled?
  3. When is the month considered closed?
  4. What reports will I receive?
  5. How are questions and unusual transactions handled?
  6. Is cleanup included or separate?
  7. What causes the price or scope to change?
  8. What work will still remain with me?

Summer Peaks' model

The goal is to fit the accounting support to the business: monthly bookkeeping when that is all you need, cleanup when the records require it, and additional AP/AR, reporting, cash-flow or process support only when those services solve a real problem.
