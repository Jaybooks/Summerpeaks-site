How Much Does Outsourced Bookkeeping Really Cost?

What drives outsourced bookkeeping pricing, how to compare providers, and how to think about cost versus an internal hire.

There is no useful universal bookkeeping price. Two businesses with the same revenue can have very different transaction volume, account counts, payroll, receivables, payables, inventory, cleanup needs, and reporting expectations.

What usually affects price?

Common workload drivers include transaction volume, number of bank and card accounts, invoicing and bill-pay responsibilities, payroll complexity, inventory, sales channels, entity structure, reporting needs, and the quality of the existing books. Frequency and responsiveness requirements matter too.

Monthly support versus cleanup work

Ongoing bookkeeping assumes a reasonably stable starting point and repeatable monthly process. Cleanup is a project: prior periods may need reconciliation, old balances may need support, and unresolved receivables, payables, loans, payroll, or owner activity may need reconstruction. The two scopes should be distinguished clearly.

Compare total cost, not just the monthly fee

Consider what the owner or staff must still do after hiring the provider. A low fee can be less attractive if the business continues assembling reports, chasing every transaction question, managing collections, or repairing recurring errors internally.

What should a quote include?

A useful proposal should identify the services included, frequency, expected client responsibilities, reporting deliverables, treatment of cleanup or catch-up work, communication expectations, and what would trigger a scope change. Clear boundaries make competing offers easier to compare.

Price follows scope.

The most useful comparison starts with the outcome and responsibilities, then evaluates the fee attached to that work.
