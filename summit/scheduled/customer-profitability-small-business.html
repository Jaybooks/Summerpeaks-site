Customer Profitability: How to Know Which Clients Are Actually Making You Money

A practical guide to customer profitability using revenue, direct labor, materials, contractors, service effort and disciplined treatment of shared overhead.

Revenue rankings tell you who buys the most. They do not tell you who creates the best economic result. Customer profitability can help with pricing, staffing, scope and sales decisions when the underlying cost data is reliable enough to compare.

Start with contribution, not arbitrary overhead

Compare customer revenue with costs that can be reasonably traced to serving that customer: direct labor, contractors, materials, freight, commissions or other meaningful direct costs.

Measure service effort

Some customers create rework, support time, collections effort or scope creep that never appears in the original price. Track only the effort that can be measured consistently enough to change a decision.

Do not allocate every overhead dollar just because you can

Artificial precision can mislead. Shared rent, executive salaries and broad administrative costs may be better evaluated at the company level unless there is a sensible allocation driver.

Use four buckets

High contribution/high strategic value; high contribution/low strategic value; low contribution/high strategic value; low contribution/low strategic value. The goal is not automatically to fire low-margin customers; it is to understand why they are low and whether price, process or scope can change.

Review trends, not one unusual month

A single project or onboarding period can distort the picture. Look for repeated patterns before changing the relationship.
