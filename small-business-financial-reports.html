Financial reporting should answer management questions. A long report package is not useful if the owner cannot quickly understand what changed, why it changed, and what requires attention.

PROFITABILITY

Profit & Loss

Shows revenue, direct costs, operating expenses, and profit over a defined period.

POSITION

Balance Sheet

Shows what the business owns, what it owes, and the resulting equity at a point in time.

COLLECTIONS

Accounts Receivable Aging

Shows customer balances by age so overdue receivables are visible.

OBLIGATIONS

Accounts Payable Aging

Shows vendor obligations and helps management plan outgoing cash.

Cash deserves its own view

Profit and cash are not the same thing. A business can be profitable while cash is constrained by slow collections, inventory, debt payments, or timing differences.

Compare against something

Reports become more useful when compared with the prior month, prior year, budget, or another meaningful benchmark. The change often matters more than the isolated number.

The best report ends with a decision.

If a report does not help management decide what to do next, it may be reporting activity rather than providing insight.
