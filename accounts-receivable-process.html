Accounts receivable problems often begin before an invoice is overdue. The best AR process starts with clear terms, accurate billing, easy payment options, and defined follow-up responsibilities.

Step 1 — Make the invoice easy to understand

Use consistent invoice dates, due dates, customer names, service descriptions, payment instructions, and contact information. Confusion delays payment.

Step 2 — Define the follow-up timeline

Decide what happens before the due date, on the due date, after 15 days, after 30 days, and at later escalation points. A documented timeline makes collections consistent.

Step 3 — Separate disputes from nonpayment

A customer who believes an invoice is wrong needs a different workflow than a customer who simply has not paid. Track disputes separately so they can be resolved quickly.

Step 4 — Make ownership obvious

Someone should own the next action on every material overdue balance. Visibility without ownership is not a collections process.

Step 5 — Review AR as a management metric

Track aging, average days to payment, large balances, repeat late payers, and the portion of receivables concentrated in a few customers.
