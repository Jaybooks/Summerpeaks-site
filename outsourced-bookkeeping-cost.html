What drives outsourced bookkeeping pricing, how to compare providers, and how to think about cost versus an internal hire.

There is no useful single price for outsourced bookkeeping because two businesses with the same revenue can create very different workloads. The better question is: what work needs to be done, how often, and how much complexity exists behind the numbers?

What usually affects price?

Transaction volume matters, but so do the number of bank and credit accounts, payroll activity, accounts receivable, accounts payable, reporting requirements, cleanup needs, and the condition of the current books.

Monthly support versus cleanup work

Ongoing monthly work is usually more predictable. Cleanup and catch-up engagements often require more investigation because the goal is not just recording transactions—it is determining what happened and rebuilding reliable records.

Good pricing should follow the workload.

A business should not have to buy a large package simply because it needs professional support.

Compare total cost, not just the monthly fee

When comparing outsourced support to an employee, consider salary or hourly pay, payroll taxes, benefits, recruiting, supervision, training, turnover risk, software, and the amount of productive accounting work actually required.

What should a quote include?

A strong quote should define the scope, recurring responsibilities, reporting expectations, communication model, any cleanup work, and what happens when the workload changes.
